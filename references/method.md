# Populate and explore a useful strategy tree

The map should contribute useful thinking before asking the person for more work. Use recovered facts, constraints and prior decisions to build concrete branches. Where knowledge is incomplete, contribute useful possibilities and label them as proposals or hypotheses. An empty list of headings is not a first map. Concise presentation means progressive disclosure, not withholding options, evidence or deeper reasoning.

## Follow the user's mode

- **Explore:** generate a substantive breadth of mechanisms, methods and concrete candidate ideas, including useful alternatives beyond the first obvious packages. Populate several ideas under meaningful groups and deeper variants where context supports them. Keep the map open; do not impose a one-winner decision, rank options or use a residual branch as a substitute for generating ideas.
- **Deepen:** follow the branch the user chooses. Develop its variants, relevant examples, how it works, dependencies, counterpoints and further possibilities. Branch research should enrich this subtree, including discovering possibilities absent from the initial map. A focus is a temporary view, not a decision to discard every other branch. Return to the overview with the broader map intact.
- **Compare or converge:** when the user asks or signals readiness, agree the choice scope and compare relevant alternatives on common criteria. A focused branch is not automatically the preferred option. Move back into exploration whenever a new possibility or reframing warrants it.

The current conversational agent owns synthesis and continuity. Bounded research agents can investigate independent mechanisms, source examples or missing knowledge; integrate their evidence and newly discovered alternatives into the canonical tree rather than keeping detached summaries. No fixed number of ideas, research rounds or stages is required.

## Shape the reasoning

Work from first principles: recover the goal or decision, the relevant situation and constraints, then distinguish what is observed from what is assumed. For each developed possibility, explain the causal mechanism: what would change, why that could help the desired outcome, and which condition could break the link. Cite evidence that supports or challenges those steps; do not substitute a source list, familiar framework or successful format for the argument. Early possibilities may have tentative mechanisms or named gaps rather than invented facts. A conclusion must be no stronger than the reasoning and evidence beneath it.

For content, **purpose → reader situation → desired change → obstacle → contribution → post possibilities** can be a useful reasoning sequence. Adapt or omit steps that do not fit: personal purpose can include reflection, connection or enjoyment. These are not six sibling content types or a mandatory intake wizard. Lore supplies credible material and perspective; a reader connection explains relevance; the proposed point of view joins them. Broad topic curation or copying a popular post does not establish that connection.

Choose a structure suited to the current question. These are examples, not mandatory templates:

| Question | Useful branches and deeper content |
| --- | --- |
| Brainstorm possibilities | Provisional groups by one primary mechanism or reader/customer job; several concrete ideas beneath each, then variants, examples and relevant dependencies. Distinguish brainstorming groups from mutually exclusive implementation choices. |
| Compare options | Mutually exclusive alternatives within the stated scope; beneath each, the same relevant criteria, trade-offs, what must be true and evidence that would change the choice. |
| Diagnose a problem | Possible explanations grouped on one basis; beneath each, observations that support or disconfirm it and an evidence check that distinguishes competing explanations. Mark interacting causes and incomplete coverage explicitly. |
| Develop a strategy | Concrete choices towards the stated outcome; beneath them, the constraints, capabilities or conditions each depends on, and the next evidence check. Separate choice dimensions into levels when they can combine. |

Keep prior decisions distinct from hypotheses. In notes, identify the basis as a recorded decision, observation, inference, assumption or proposed possibility; use the existing schema rather than inventing node kinds. A proposed option can be `open`; an untested causal hypothesis should be `uncertain`. A status alone never establishes evidence.

### Check the first split of a choice tree

When the root asks which option to pursue, its direct children must answer that question. Shared criteria, constraints, evidence boundaries and parking groups belong in session context or root notes; option-specific analysis belongs beneath the option. Check the saved root children themselves before showing the canvas. Explaining a mixed level in its notes does not fix what the diagram shows.

For example, a bookshop choosing a use for its spare room could start with:

```text
Use of the spare room
├─ Paid workshops
├─ Quiet reading room
└─ Keep as storage
```

The shared five-hour staffing limit and unknown budget go in context, with their implications beneath each option. Do not add sibling cards called `Shared criteria`, `Evidence boundary` or `Proposed extensions`. A shared timetable can be another clearly proposed option if useful; adding it does not establish exhaustive coverage. This rule concerns a choice tree, not every diagnostic or brainstorming structure.

Populate beyond the first split where context supports it. During exploration, add distinct methods and concrete possibilities; during deepening, add mechanisms, evidence, counterpoints and relevant checks. Follow the user's focus; during comparison, prioritise uncertainty that could reverse the choice. Keep a small visible map while retaining useful deeper content and notes. Preserve discarded options as `ruled-out` with their reason and reconsideration condition; do not delete history merely to tidy the view.

## Use existing evidence before new trials

Research both discovery and uncertainty: what methods or examples are missing, what distinctions improve the map, and what evidence could change a branch or eventual choice? Search relevant current expert guidance, primary sources and real implementations before asking the user to invent or run another experiment. Inspect actual examples and linked sources where available; state access limits rather than treating a search summary as inspection.

- Separate an example's job or intended outcome, its format, its delivery and promotion, and its evidence of success. An available resource, public price or popular post does not itself establish paid demand or business impact.
- Use the documented optional `sources` registry, node `sourceIds` and `provenance` to distinguish observed examples, proposals, catalogue concepts and context; preserve legacy source text. Evidence nodes and notes explain the finding. Record direct source identity and relevant date, what was observed or reported, access limits, applicability and material opposing evidence. Distinguish a resource directly promoted in a post from a related mention or an author-comment offer. Label inference separately; reported results are not independently verified results.
- Add, split or regroup ideas when research discovers a useful mechanism or distinction; do not use research only to score the original shortlist. Revise comparisons or recommendations only when the conversation is at that stage. Put evidence and separate fit inferences into the relevant branches and notes. Keep cross-cutting evidence and criteria separate from partitions; refer to affected branches in notes and explicitly bound any taxonomy.
- Propose new data collection or an experiment when consequential uncertainty remains and existing evidence cannot reasonably resolve it. Explain what remains unknown and what result would change the choice. Local outcomes may still need testing; existing examples reduce uncertainty without guaranteeing transfer.

For post-led exploration, follow the chain **actual post → specifically identified resource → classified mechanism → proposed adaptations**. Inspect the post and linked resource separately; record when only an introduction, promotion or gated preview was available. A post may support several branches, but preserve one source identity and reference it rather than presenting duplicates as independent examples. A catalogue entry establishes an idea in a catalogue, not a produced asset. Mark adapted ideas as proposals until their own evidence exists.

Keep source evidence distinct from result evidence. A visible native post establishes what it shows; comments, enrolments or access requests do not establish attributable revenue or successful implementation. A failed or blocked embed does not erase the source: retain its link and verified interpretation, and state the display limitation. Verify actual rendered post content before claiming a host supports the peek; an iframe load event alone is insufficient.

## Scoped MECE check

Apply this check to every decomposition before presentation and after updates. Its siblings must be **mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive within an explicit scope**, or visibly identify the unresolved boundaries or gaps. First identify the relationship: a partition divides one universe; a sequence orders steps; a causal link proposes influence; support and reference links connect evidence or shared items. Do not imply every parent-child edge is a partition, that sequential stages are alternatives, or that separate causes act independently.

Peer navigation groups also need one coherent inclusion rule. Calling mixed branches “complementary dimensions” or “not a partition” does not repair a hierarchy that puts what, where, how and individual items at the same organising level. Factor those dimensions into scoped levels or explicit questions, and attach support/reference relationships where they apply. Check the underlying records or choices, not merely whether each supplied heading string was filed once. Keep uncertain items visible while repairing the grouping; a provisional label does not excuse a known structural mixture.

1. State the parent question and bounded universe: what items or choices are being divided, over what horizon, and what is excluded? Choose one grouping basis that answers that question, not a fixed framework carried over from another case.
2. Define each sibling's inclusion and exclusion boundaries at comparable abstraction. Do not mix dimensions such as audience, channel, format, status or evidence, a broad category with its subset, an option with its cause, or a criterion with one answer to it.
3. Test overlap with actual items and plausible boundary cases: can one item meet two definitions? Rename, regroup, factor dimensions into levels or define a primary-placement rule. Do not hide overlap by duplicating the item or discarding an inconvenient possibility.
4. Test coverage against the defined universe, known facts and plausible omitted cases: what fits nowhere? Research can expose missing categories or change their boundaries. Record specific unresolved cases or evidence gaps; an Other/unknown/hybrid bucket exposes them but does not prove substantive completeness.

For an exploratory classification, assign each canonical item one primary home under the stated basis and record secondary mechanisms or affected branches as references/tags in notes. Preserve its identity when referenced elsewhere and deduplicate shared source evidence; another placement is not independent proof. If primary placement remains ambiguous, regroup or visibly mark the ambiguity. A hybrid can have an explicit home with references to its components, or factor into separate dimensions; do not present it as independent of those components. Evidence and criteria attached to an idea support it rather than compete with it as categories.

One primary assignment is a deduplication rule, not proof of conceptual MECE. Alternative exploratory treatments can overlap or combine when they answer the same question at a consistent level; say which relationships are variants or references rather than claiming exclusive choices. Keep an unanalysed input visibly unanalysed until its mechanism is clear. Renaming a hodgepodge or switching its edge labels does not resolve its organising defect.

When actions can combine, separate decision dimensions. Once the user wants a bounded implementation choice, exclusive alternatives such as “A only”, “B only”, “A and B”, and “neither” may help if they exhaust that choice; they are not a default brainstorming restriction. Do not discard a viable combination merely to simplify the picture. When causes interact, distinct categories do not make their effects independent: record dependencies in notes and say when a tree alone is insufficient.

Divergent ideas may be collected provisionally before structuring; this check must not restrict their breadth or force a winner. If overlap or coverage is unresolved, mark the split's parent `uncertain`, visibly identify it as provisional, and put the specific gap in its notes. Do not claim MECE until the semantic check passes. Schema validation checks structural validity, not the truth of boundaries or completeness. Keep user-facing explanations brief; record the parent question, basis and boundary reasoning mainly in notes, with the existing fields.

## Keep review claims current

Review the actual saved content before calling reasoning reviewed. Record the inspected snapshot/revision, affected question or subtree, governing context and sources, concrete check findings, unresolved points and who or what performed the review. A filled field, unique IDs, `supported` status or an agent's assertion that it complied is not a review. The findings must address the mechanism, sibling boundaries, overlap, coverage and evidence limits. A review can judge an exploration coherent while leaving it provisional; that does not certify an exhaustive MECE partition or support a recommendation.

Recheck when relevant meaning changes: the goal/scope/basis, candidate or conclusion, a sibling's membership, a node's substantive label/notes/status, an edge role, assumptions, source content/limitations or governing context. Invalidate affected decompositions and dependent conclusions, including dependencies reached through references. If dependency tracking is unavailable or relevance is uncertain, conservatively treat the review as stale until rechecked. Pan, focus and collapse alone do not change reasoning.

Use the host's real review path when one exists; do not inject client-authored reviewed flags or copy an earlier receipt onto new content. Without such a path, keep an explicit bounded review note and communicate its limits; do not invent a trusted review service. Legacy, incomplete and failed-review maps stay readable, editable and exportable as unreviewed/draft, with specific gaps visible. Review is an authoring responsibility, not a new user-consent ceremony or a demand for another experiment. The bundled runtime validates structure, not semantic quality or automatic review freshness. Exports carry the review's snapshot scope; later offline reasoning edits do not inherit its validity.

## Compare when ready

When the user is ready to compare, show which alternatives remain open or are ruled out and why. Identify evidence or changed conditions that would alter a supported preference. Ask only for the highest-impact missing or changed information; otherwise continue the useful work in the user's current mode.

Saving does not require convergence. Preserve the populated exploration, including the developed subtree, keeping the recommendation empty or explicitly deferred while no choice has been made. When a decision is reached, capture its rationale, unresolved risks and agreed next action. Use the entrypoint's tree-first exports in either case.
